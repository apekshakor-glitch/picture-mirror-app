Prompt Mirror

How to play
  1. We'll generate a picture with AI — but we keep the prompt that made it a secret.
  2. Study the picture, then write your own prompt describing what you see.
  3. We generate a new picture from your prompt.
  4. An AI judge compares your picture to the original and scores how close you got, out of 100.

Each round makes one call to write the hidden prompt, two image-generation calls, and one scoring call to OpenAI, which uses your API credits.

How it's judged

An AI art judge compares your recreated picture to the hidden original across several dimensions: the exact subject (and how many of them), the color palette, the composition and framing, the art style and medium (photo vs. painting vs. cartoon, etc.), the setting and background, and small distinguishing details like lighting, texture, or accessories. It's deliberately strict and uses the full 0–100 scale, so scores in the 90s are reserved for near-perfect matches — even one clearly wrong detail (a different color, a missing prop) costs several points. It never judges how good your picture looks on its own, only how closely it matches the original.

Emoji Charades

How to play
  1. AI invents a fresh hidden answer — a movie title, an idiom, or a common phrase — and translates it into a string of emojis.
  2. The category (Movie, Idiom, or Phrase) is shown right alongside the emojis, so you always know what kind of answer to look for.
  3. You study the emojis and type your best guess. Scoring is instant and local — no extra AI call, no images, just a close look at how well your guess matches.

Each round makes one AI call to OpenAI (to invent the answer and translate it into emojis), which uses your API credits. A fresh answer is generated every round, so content doesn't repeat.

How it's judged

Scoring is done locally, without any AI call. Your guess is compared to the hidden answer two ways: how many of its key words you got right (ignoring small words like "a" or "the"), and how close your spelling and phrasing are letter-for-letter. The two are blended — weighted a bit more toward key words — into a score out of 100. An exact match is always 100; a guess that shares no words or letters with the answer scores near 0.

Code Mirror

How to play
  1. We'll ask AI to write a small Python function — but we keep the request that produced it a secret.
  2. Study the function, then write your own prompt describing what you think it does.
  3. We generate a new function from your prompt.
  4. An AI code reviewer compares your function to the original and scores how closely it matches, out of 100. Nothing is ever run — it's judged by reading the code, like a human reviewer would.

Each round makes two code-generation calls and one scoring call to OpenAI, which uses your API credits.

How it's judged

An AI code reviewer reads your recreated function side-by-side with the hidden original — nothing is ever executed. It judges whether the two would behave the same way on typical and edge-case inputs (empty input, negatives, duplicates, and so on), whether they take a similar algorithmic approach, whether the function's signature and overall structure match, how robust each is (error handling, input validation), and how similar the readability and style are. It's strict: scores above 90 are reserved for functionally equivalent code, while a behavioral mismatch or a completely different approach to the problem scores below 20.

Prompt Repair Shop

How to play
  1. A customer hands you a badly written AI prompt, a plain-English requirement it needs to meet, and the one real message it has to handle.
  2. Edit the prompt, then click "Test repair" to run it against that message and see whether the result meets the requirement.
  3. You get 3 attempts. Editing is free; only clicking "Test repair" uses one up — and a technical/API error never costs you an attempt.
  4. Solve it faster for a higher score: 100 points on attempt 1, 75 on attempt 2, 50 on attempt 3. Hints are optional and cost 10 points each.
How it's judged

Your edited prompt is run once against a real customer message, and the AI's response is checked against the ticket's requirement. Solving it on your first attempt scores 100, your second 75, your third 50 — not solving it within 3 attempts scores 0. Each hint you use subtracts 10 points from whatever you scored.
